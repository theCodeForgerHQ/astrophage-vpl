## 2. The +8.2 % temperature bias — cause found **[O-verified]**

### Block D's premise was false

`Cell.truth_eedf` flows `grid.py:_truth_eedf_factory` → `build_channels(truth_eedf_factory=)`
→ consumed at `channels.py:1010` by the **truth-side OES instrument only**. The
inversion-side OES (`channels.py:1018`) is always Maxwellian, and LIF, Thomson and
interferometry never receive the factory at all.

The baseline ablates OES. Therefore flipping `DRUYVESTEYN ↔ MAXWELLIAN` is a **bit-for-bit
no-op** in this configuration — confirmed by direct run (identical to all printed digits)
and by independent code trace. The EEDF hypothesis cannot explain the bias here.

### The actual cause: genuine L1↔L0 model discrepancy

Same cell, same seeds, same knobs; only the truth model changes.

| Configuration | Tier | T_e error, seed 0 | T_e error, seed 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 truth → L0 inversion** | 1 | **+0.0048 %** | **+0.0057 %** |
| **L1 truth → L0 inversion** | 2 | **+7.903 %** | **+8.424 %** |

**[O-verified]** — table above re-run by the orchestrator. The executing agent's wider
5-seed L0→L0 sweep gave mean T_e error 0.0003 %, SD 0.0044 %.

The bias is not reduced when the models match — it is annihilated to numerical noise. Ruled
out alongside it: calibration (bias unchanged at 8.31 % with `calibration_uncertainty=False`,
a diagnostic-only configuration that must not be reported), a `T_e` prior or bound (the same
prior recovers `T_e` to 0.0003 % when the model matches), and LIF being broken against its
own generative model (LIF is active in the L0→L0 run that recovers `T_e` exactly).

**Mechanism.** LIF is the only channel reading a local ion drift velocity `u_i`, and its
measurement volume is pinned to an **L0-derived** grid node — the outermost node, "where the
L0 profile has relaxed to the quasineutral presheath and the drift is exactly the Bohm
speed". L0's `u_i` is a ballistic, pressure-free trajectory (`physics/analytic/sheath.py`);
L1's comes from a coupled Newton solve with an ion pressure-gradient term
(`physics/fluid/sheath.py`) on a graded mesh. That node is not the same physical point in
L1's presheath-to-sheath transition. LIF faithfully reports L1's `u_i` there; the L0
inversion back-solves `T_e` from `c_s` assuming it is the Bohm point, and absorbs the
mismatch into `T_e`.

Corroborated by the ablation table: the bias is +8 % in every row containing LIF and flips
to −4.86 % in the row that excludes it.

### The sheath-edge offset diagnostic — the mechanism is now quantitative

Run on the 5x5 discrepancy grid (`DISCREPANCY_SPAN=(0.8, 1.2)` in `n_0`/`T_e` factors about
RP-1), all 25 points converged.

**First: the two definitions are directly comparable.** Both models use the identical
criterion `(n_i - n_e)/n_i >= edge_tolerance`, searched inward from the bulk, outermost
crossing taken (`fluid/sheath.py:609`, and `lif/instrument.py:216` for the same convention).
So this is a like-for-like measurement, not an apples-to-oranges one.

**Second, and not previously understood: the LIF node is not either model's sheath edge.**
`channels.py:995` pins it once, at RP-1, to the last point of a fixed observation grid — a
fixed `z = 2.2799 mm`, i.e. "ten times RP-1's L0 sheath thickness", for *every* condition in
the sweep. It only sits outside the sheath because the 0.8-1.2 span never grows `s(θ)` far
enough to reach it.

| Quantity | Mean | SD | CV | Driven by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offset (absolute) | 598.5 µm | 53.3 µm | **8.91 %** | `T_e` (r ≈ 1.00) |
| Offset / λ_D | 14.644 | 0.278 | 1.90 % | `T_e` (r = −0.997) |
| Offset / s₀ | 0.5210 | 0.0458 | **8.79 %** | `T_e` (r ≈ 1.00) |
| **`u_i(L1)/c_s` at the LIF node** | **1.0538** | 0.0115 | — | `n_0` (r = −0.97) |
| **Excess (ratio − 1)** | **0.0538** | 0.0115 | **21.4 %** | `n_0` |

**Verdict: the offset drifts. There is no single correct position to retune to.** Two
independent condition-dependent drivers are visible — a `T_e`-driven positional drift and an
`n_0`-driven velocity-ratio drift, nearly orthogonal to each other. All correlations exceed
|r| = 0.97, so these are deterministic trends, not scatter.

### The arithmetic closes

LIF reads `u_i` at the fixed node; the inversion assumes that value *is* `c_s`, and
`c_s ∝ sqrt(T_e)`, so the recovered temperature inflates by the **square** of the velocity
excess:

    T_e_hat / T_e_true = (u_i / c_s)^2

At the high-`n_0` edge of the grid (`n_0` factor 1.2, nearest the sealed truth's 1.31), the
measured ratio is 1.0386-1.0426, giving **+7.9 % to +8.7 %**.

**Observed bias across 10 seeds: +7.26 % to +9.09 %, mean +8.20 %.**

The mechanism is therefore not merely plausible — it *predicts the observed number* from an
independently measured velocity ratio. The +8.2 % is fully accounted for.

### Recommendation: discrepancy term, not repositioning — now on evidence

The earlier recommendation was made on principle ("retuning would be tuning to flatter one
configuration"). It is now made on measurement: **retuning cannot work, because the required
correction is a two-parameter function of plasma condition, not a constant.** A single
repositioning would be correct at one operating point and wrong everywhere else.

A model-discrepancy term on LIF's `u_i` observable carries exactly that condition-dependence,
plus its own uncertainty. The machinery exists (`vpl.experiment.discrepancy_basis`) and is
trustworthy as of Block G's full-rank verification.

### But the existing discrepancy machinery is the wrong shape for this **[O-verified]**

`coherent_discrepancy` is **variance-only. It cannot shift a mean.**

Traced: `lif/instrument.py:485` documents it as "per-sample **standard deviation** of model
discrepancy"; at `:545-546` it is appended to `rows`; `rows` becomes `basis` via
`stack_coherent_rows`; `basis` is passed to `coherent_gaussian_log_prob(residual=obs.values -
predicted, variance=..., basis=basis)`. **The residual is untouched** — the basis only adds a
low-rank term to the covariance.

So the existing term can widen LIF's interval but cannot correct its prediction. It
**structurally cannot remove the +8.2 % bias.**

There is a second-order effect that will look like a fix and is not one: inflating LIF's
variance reduces how strongly LIF pulls the joint fit, which shifts the MAP indirectly —
the same mechanism as the OES weighting. So applying a large LIF discrepancy probably
*will* reduce the temperature bias, **by silencing the project's best channel.** That is a
bad trade wearing the costume of a correction, and the diagnostic for telling them apart is
the interval width: a genuine correction leaves it roughly unchanged, silencing balloons it.

**What is actually needed** is a *mean* correction — a condition-dependent multiplicative
factor on the predicted LIF observable, of magnitude `u_i/c_s` = 1.0386-1.0744 as measured
above. That is a new capability, not a configuration change: the discrepancy basis would have
to carry an offset alongside its standard deviation, and the likelihood would have to apply
it to `predicted` rather than to the covariance.

Independently re-traced by a second agent, which agreed and added the exact algebra:
`coherent_gaussian_log_prob` (`instruments/coherent.py:152-201`) computes
`log N(r; 0, D + U'U)` by Woodbury. `coherent_discrepancy` only ever contributes to `U`.
The mean is pinned at zero by construction.

### The correction is already being measured — and then discarded **[O-verified]**

`discrepancy_basis.py:587` builds each basis row as `high_values - low_values` — the
**signed** difference between the two models' predictions. Verified directly.

So the systematic *direction* of the L1-L0 disagreement is computed, per channel, at all 25
grid points. It is then handed to `U`, where `Sigma = D + U'U` makes it sign-blind: a
quadratic form cannot tell `+delta` from `-delta`. **The exact quantity a mean correction
needs already exists and is thrown away one step before it could be used.**

This changes the cost of the fix. It is not "go measure a systematic offset" — that is done.
It is "stop discarding the sign, and apply it to the prediction."

**Where the change would go.** Not in `likelihood()` — in `forward()`. A `theta`-conditioned
interpolant over the 5x5 grid, evaluated at each trial `theta` during the MAP search and
added to LIF's predicted observable before the residual is formed
(`_LifOnReconstructedIvdf.forward` or `LifInstrument.forward`). It cannot be a single
subtracted mean row, because §2 established the required correction is a two-parameter
function of `(n_0, T_e)` with near-orthogonal drivers.

### Measured: variance inflation cannot touch the bias, across 15 orders of magnitude **[O-run]**

The theoretical argument above says a variance term cannot shift a mean. It was then tested
directly, by scaling a rank-1 LIF discrepancy row over fifteen orders of magnitude and
watching the inversion. Seed 0, `ablate="oes"`, `model_discrepancy=True`.

| LIF row `mean\|row\|` | Flux error | T_e error | Half-width |
|---|---|---|---|
| *(no discrepancy at all)* | 1.3757 % | 7.9038 % | 0.005925 |
| 0.034 | 1.3757 % | 7.9038 % | 0.005925 |
| 3.36e4 | 1.3757 % | 7.9038 % | 0.005925 |
| 3.36e10 | 1.3591 % | 7.9002 % | 0.009348 |
| 8.39e12 (250x larger) | 1.3591 % | 7.9002 % | 0.009349 |

*(calibration-uncertainty OFF here — diagnostic only, never reportable — to isolate the
discrepancy row from the calibration row.)*

Three regimes, and the mechanism is **not** broken: below ~1e5 the row is negligible; above
~1e10 it saturates, because the direction is by then effectively infinitely uncertain and
projected out entirely; between them there is a real transition.

**But saturation moves the temperature bias by 0.004 percentage points** — 7.9038 % to
7.9002 %. Taking LIF's model uncertainty along that direction all the way to infinity does
not dent an 8.2 % bias. The interval widens (0.0059 → 0.0093) and the bias does not move.
That is variance inflation behaving exactly as the algebra says it must.

**Methodological note worth keeping.** The original sweep used rows of 1.68e9, 8.39e9 and
3.36e10 — i.e. **three points on the saturated plateau**. It returned three near-identical
rows, which reads as "no effect" but was actually "no *resolution*". That was only caught by
asking whether a null result meant *no effect* or *not wired up*, and testing both ends. A
sweep that varies a parameter across a plateau looks like a clean negative and is not one.

**Integration check:** `run_cell(discrepancy_path=)` still works after Block G's return-type
change. `save_channel_discrepancy` and `load_channel_discrepancy` both still speak the bare
mapping that `build_channels(discrepancy=)` expects, and no caller passes a raw
`DiscrepancySweepResult` into either. Verified by live smoke test. No integration bug.

### Recommendation: document, do not fix

This is what the T2 tier exists to produce — a mismatched-physics configuration exposing a
quantifiable model-discrepancy signature, isolated to one channel's forward-model
assumption, with a clean on/off control proving it is neither noise nor calibration nor a
numerical artefact.

Do **not** retune LIF's `z_index` pinning or the `gamma_i`/`h_l` conventions to chase it to
zero. That would be tuning the analytic model to flatter one measured configuration — the
anti-pattern the codebase's own docstrings repeatedly warn against. The low-risk principled
route is a model-discrepancy term on LIF's `u_i` observable, for which the machinery already
exists (`vpl.experiment.discrepancy_basis`).

## 3. Block C — per-channel likelihood weights (implemented)

`vpl.inverse.fusion`: `Channel.weight`, `JointLikelihood.with_weights`,
`FusionDetail.weights`. `vpl.experiment.grid`: `run_cell(channel_weights=)` and
`CellReport.channel_weights`.

Scaling a Gaussian log-likelihood by `w` inflates its variance by exactly `1/w`, so
`w = 1/(understatement factor)²`. This is exact algebra, not an approximation, and it is a
named peer-reviewed technique — the **power likelihood** (Ibrahim & Chen 2000,
*Statistical Science* **15** 46; Boss et al. 2025, *Biometrics* **81** ujaf008).

Guarantees, all tested (28 tests in `test_fusion.py`, plus 267 in `vpl-inverse` and 28 in
`test_grid.py`, all passing; `ruff` clean):

- Weight 1.0 is **bit-for-bit** identical to the pre-weighting path, so every number
  measured before this change stays comparable with every number after it.
- `w < 1` strictly widens: verified as a strict reduction in the log-likelihood gap between
  a good and a bad state.
- Weight 0 or negative is **refused**. Zero would exclude a channel invisibly — doc 01
  IF-6's "formed and small" — whereas the detection gate records exclusions. Negative would
  reward disagreement.
- Applied weights are carried into `CellReport`, because a weighted run and an unweighted
  one are otherwise indistinguishable after the fact.

**Honest limitation, recorded in the docstring.** Integrated Data Analysis practice
(Fischer et al. 2003 *PPCF* **45** 1095; Minerva, Svensson & Werner) infers per-channel
discrepancy parameters *jointly* with the plasma state, or selects the tempering exponent
adaptively. A weight fixed once from an audit is the weaker point-estimate form. The
report must say "point-estimate power-likelihood correction, calibrated once against a
specific noise model", and `w` must be re-derived if the noise model or operating point
changes.

### This overturns the plan's stated root cause

The recovery plan §1.3 says "every channel understates its own uncertainty; fusion compounds

mildly *conservative* (0.42–0.81x) on the parameters they can actually see. A uniform

### And it independently reproduces the ablation

The audit was performed with no reference to the ablation table. It concludes OES should be
down-weighted by `w = 1/28.6² ≈ 1.2e-3` (or `1/137.9² ≈ 5.3e-5` on the T_e factor) — that
is, suppressed by three to five orders of magnitude, which is *operationally the same thing
as dropping it*.

The ablation found empirically that dropping OES takes the result from 31.74 % / 0-3 covered
to 4.75 % / 8-10 covered. **A principled error budget and a brute-force ablation, derived
independently, agree on which channel is the problem.** That agreement is the strongest
evidence in the project that the LIF-led configuration is a justified choice rather than a
number picked because it flattered.

### Why OES is bad is worse than "overconfident"

Under honest calibration, OES alone is not merely narrow — it is frequently *wrong*:

- **8 of 20 seeds produced a non-positive-definite Laplace Hessian** — no claimed interval
  exists at all. The "claims ±1.05 %" is a mean over the 12 seeds that produced one.
- The MAP point itself is badly biased: mean n̂₀ 33 % low, mean T̂_e **189 % high** — while
  `MapResult.converged=True` on all 20.

So the 28.6x/137.9x figures are contaminated by MAP non-robustness rather than being clean
noise-scatter measurements. **Treat them as a lower bound on how bad OES-alone is.** This
reproduces and sharpens `channels.py`'s own documented warning that a single OES line cannot
identify plasma density under honest calibration.

### Consequences for Block C

- **Weight OES down; leave the other three alone.** The evidence supports nothing else.
- OES's understatement is **anisotropic** (28.6x on n₀, 137.9x on T_e) and `Channel.weight`
  is a single scalar, so one weight cannot correct both. Taking the larger factor is
  conservative but nearly zeroes the channel. This is a judgement call and must be stated,

**Thomson: confirmed and stable.** Both truth states land in [0.77, 0.88]; the executor's

### The MAP-robustness claim is confirmed, and it is worse than reported

The reviewer reproduced it independently: **15/15 OES fits report `converged=True`**, yet
only **6 of 15 land near the true optimum**. The other nine split into 5 singular-Hessian
failures and — the part nobody had seen — **4 that land on a *wrong mode* which passes the
positive-definiteness check**. Those produce a tight, confident interval around the wrong
answer, which is strictly worse than refusing to produce one.

Worst cases: `n̂₀ −75 %, T̂_e +644 %`, positive-definite, `converged=True`. Aggregate bias
n₀ −31.1 %, T_e +184.7 % — landing almost exactly on the executor's independent −33 %/+189 %.

**Consequence: OES's factor is not a clean noise-scatter measurement.** It is the scatter of
a bimodal optimiser, mixing "found the right mode" with "found a wrong one, confidently", at
roughly 40/60 odds. Any OES factor must be labelled as such.

### Why two honest measurements of the same factor differ

The reviewer's Thomson/OES contrast explains the live 5x LIF disagreement (0.112 vs 0.60) as
well. Thomson's likelihood is unimodal and always positive-definite — its factor is a
*stable statistic*. OES's is multimodal, so its factor depends on what fraction of a small-N
draw happened to find the right mode, and that fraction is itself noisy at N=15-20. LIF's
only stochasticity is a single coherent calibration draw, a low-dimensional and
non-smooth nuisance — analogously unstable at small N.

**The general rule, worth stating in the report: a channel's understatement factor is only
as stable as its likelihood surface is well-behaved.** Thomson's is. OES's and LIF's are not.

Density scatter (SD ±5.08 %) — not either bias — is what limits the headline error. Two
supporting facts made a hypothesis attractive: `LIF.scale_uncertainty` is **5.0 %** and LIF's
density scale is limited by it; while Thomson and interferometry are **absolutely calibrated**
and were measured as *conservative* (overstating their own uncertainty). Up-weighting them
should let them pull harder and break the degeneracy.

**It was measured, and it is wrong.** `L1→L0`, cal ON, `ablate="oes"`, seeds 0-9. Baseline
reproduced the published numbers exactly, so the setup is validated.

| `interferometry: 1.300` | 4.7054 % | **±5.085 %** | 6.255 % | 8/10 |

### Why it failed — conservative does not mean underused

The error was treating "overstates its uncertainty" as "has information we are not using".
Compare what each channel actually delivers on density:

| Channel | Claims | Actually scatters |
|---|---|---|
| Fused baseline | — | **±5.08 %** |
| Thomson | ±11.54 % | ±8.76 % |
| Interferometry (post-fix) | ±10.67 % | ±9.36 % |

**Thomson and interferometry are honest but imprecise.** Both scatter ~9 % on density — worse
than the ±5.08 % the fusion already achieves. Up-weighting them cannot add precision the
fusion does not already have; it can only import their noise, and (since `w > 1` deflates
their variance) it asserts they are *better* than they claim, which amplifies any residual
per-seed bias. Thomson's density mean did move (−2.73 → −2.39 %), so it is pulling — it is
just pulling worse.

**Consequence: ±5 % density scatter appears to be close to an information floor for this
instrument set.** LIF, limited by its 5 % metastable-fraction calibration, is already the most
precise density channel available. Reducing the scatter is therefore a *metrology* problem
(a better-calibrated LIF, or a genuinely more precise density diagnostic) rather than a
software or weighting problem. That is a real limit, and it should be stated as one.

### Interferometry's factor re-audited post-Block-F

itself by construction. Replaced: **0.877x at N=100** (0.897x at N=50 — stable), 0/100

Thomson — unimodal, well-conditioned, only mildly conservative. The dramatic 0.42x was an
artefact of the defect, not a property of the instrument.

## 4b. The LIF noise gap

**LIF's `noise` flag is a no-op.** `build_channels` threads `noise` to OES
(`channels.py:1013`), Thomson (`:1045`) and interferometry (`:1067`) — **not** to LIF.
**[O-verified]** by grep and by reading the source.

This is documented rather than hidden: `channels.py:906-909` states "The LIF channel has no
detector noise to switch: doc 04 §1 puts the eighteen sources of doc 04 §7.2 in `F4`, and
`LifInstrument.observe`'s docstring is explicit that its output is not a complete synthetic
measurement." So it is a known scoping decision, not a defect — but its consequences were
not previously drawn out.

Consequences:

1. LIF's only per-seed stochasticity is a single coherent calibration draw (5 % radiometric
   scale, 2 MHz frequency-axis registration). There is no detector-noise model to be
   over- or under-confident about.
2. Measuring "actual scatter vs claimed uncertainty" for LIF therefore measures the
   calibration-nuisance marginalisation, not a noise miscalibration. The independent
   reviewer measured LIF as **overstating** its uncertainty by ~9× on n₀ (claims ±5.04 %,
   scatters ±0.56 %) and as numerically deterministic in `T_e` — at N=3 per truth state,
   invariant across two operating points, which is expected because the driver is a fixed
   registry constant rather than physics.
3. **Do not derive LIF's Block C weight this way.** `w = 1/0.112²` would inflate an already
   conservative channel's variance ~80×, which would likely make coverage worse.

**The headline configuration runs its dominant channel without a detector-noise model.**
That belongs in the report as a stated limitation of the 4.75 % number.

Cost finding: LIF's MAP+Laplace pipeline is far more expensive than assumed — a
100-draw × 2-truth-state job did not complete in 18.5 minutes. Any future audit must budget
for this.

## 4a. Block G — defect #8 is retired **[O-verified]**

The recovery plan §2 defect #8 says "5 of 25 fluid solves in the discrepancy sweep did not
converge; up to 20 % of the basis may be built from unconverged solutions."

**That is a misreading, and the sweep has now been run to check it.** Real dolfinx-backed
sweep on the FEniCSx box, `on_nonconvergence="exclude"`, `DISCREPANCY_SPAN=(0.8, 1.2)`,
5x5 grid:

```
25/25 grid points converged, rank=25
nudged: []      failures: []
```

Five `[warning] Newton solver did not converge` lines *do* appear in the log — one before
each block of five grid points. They are not failed grid points. They are the bias
continuation's **designed adaptive step-halving**: `fluid/system.py:238-248` walks the wall
potential up to target, "each converged solution seeding the next, and any step that fails
is halved and retried from the last converged state" (`fluid/sheath.py:43`). The warning is
dolfinx reporting the step that triggered a halving; the solve then converges.

An actually-unconverged solve could not reach the basis in any case: `forms.py:408-416` sets
`error_on_nonconvergence = False` and then raises `RuntimeError` in Python — "an unconverged
iterate is not a solution and is not returned."

**Conclusion: the basis is full rank 25. No unconverged solution is in it.** The earlier
session inferred five failed solves from five log warnings; the two are different things.

### The guard rails were still worth building

Block G found something worse than the reported defect while looking for it: the sweep loop
had **no exception handling at all**. The physics layer failed loudly, but that exception
escaped with no context — which point, what theta, how many — and there was no path to a
reduced-rank basis at all. One bad point destroyed the whole estimate with an uninformative
error.

`estimate_channel_discrepancy` now returns `DiscrepancySweepResult`, and there is no code
path that returns a basis without its convergence account attached. Default mode raises
naming *every* failed point, gathered across the whole sweep rather than fail-fast.
`exclude` mode records what was dropped and the reduced rank, and refuses to return an empty
basis. A point that converges only after being nudged toward the reference is recorded as
nudged, not merged as if it were nominal. Six tests with deterministically-failing fake
solvers prove an unconverged solve cannot land silently.

quadrature over the resolved domain, plus the unresolved remainder of the 400 mm chord held

| Baseline | 3.3413e-4 |

## 5. Research

**R1 — the yardstick.** Published single-diagnostic uncertainties in low-pressure argon:
Langmuir probe n_e ±20 % vs microwave interferometry (Hopkins 1995, *J. Res. NIST* **100**
415); Thomson 3 %/6 % at n_e = 4e19 m⁻³ (van der Meiden 2008, *RSI* **79** 013505) and
~12 %/10 % more generally (Wang 2021, *RSI* **92** 123508); actinometry-based OES off by a
factor ~5 without cross-section correction (Baratte 2026, *PSST* **35** 1).

**The structural finding is more useful than any single number:** when a paper quotes a
clean single-digit uncertainty it is almost always *statistical only*; whenever authors
compare two genuinely independent methods, the discrepancy jumps to a **factor of 2–10**
(Hopkins vs interferometry; Godyak & Alexandrovich 2015 *JAP* **118** 233302 on two
interpretations of the same probe trace; Schulenberg 2021 *PSST* **30** 105003 vs PIC/MCC).
This project's own diagnosis — quoted per-channel uncertainty is statistics-only and
understates once systematics are included — is the field's normal condition, not a local
bug.

No published paper surveyed quotes an uncertainty for **ion energy flux** directly. There is
no apples-to-apples external comparator for the headline quantity. Say so.

**R2 — the probe claim must be qualified.** Our simulated probe's 3.3–3.8× density
overestimate matches the documented *uncorrected* sheath-expansion systematic (3–12×,
Frontiers in Physics 2017). But modern practice corrects it — Sheridan's model for planar
probes, regime-matched OML/Laframboise/ABR for cylindrical. No firm residual-accuracy
percentage for a *corrected* probe could be confirmed, so none should be quoted.

**Write:** "we beat an *uncorrected* probe", not "we beat a probe".

**R3 — Block C's justification is sound and named.** Prior art for multi-diagnostic joint
likelihoods is mature: Minerva (Svensson & Werner) at W7-X/JET, IDA at ASDEX-Upgrade
(Fischer 2003). Our central finding — naive fusion of mutually inconsistent channels is
worse than the best single channel — is known under three names depending on community:
conflicting evidence / Zadeh paradox (Dempster-Shafer), model misspecification and posterior
overconcentration (Miller & Dunson 2019 *JASA* **114** 527), and modular-inference feedback
contamination (Plummer 2015; Jacob et al. 2017). Fischer's own IDA tutorial lists
"underestimation of the uncertainty" from naive combination as a standard failure mode.

**Caveat on all research:** the local firecrawl stack was down (Docker not running), so both
R1 and R2 ran on the Brave fallback at abstract level rather than full text. Citations are
traceable but several numbers were not verified against primary text. **Re-run before any
of these become load-bearing in a submission.**

**R4 — still blocked.** Argon `A_ul` for 750.39/763.51 nm (gate V-24). Needs one paywalled
PDF via university access.

### 4. Unweighted OES also breaks the optimiser

MAP convergence across the unweighted rows: 5/10, 4/10, 8/10, 3/10 — against **10/10** for
the OES-dropped row and 10/10 for every weighted row. OES's multimodal likelihood does not
merely bias the answer; it destabilises the search for the whole joint inversion.

### 5. A genuine configuration choice, now quantified

| Configuration | Mean err | Temp bias | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4ch weighted (keep LIF) | **3.986 %** | +8.201 % | 8/10 |
| 3ch weighted (drop LIF) | 5.136 % | **+0.885 %** | **9/10** |

Dropping LIF costs 1.15 pp of accuracy and buys an **unbiased temperature** and **better
coverage**. That is a defensible configuration and arguably the more honest one: it has no
large unexplained systematic to caveat. Keeping LIF is the better *number*; dropping it is the
cleaner *result*. Both should be reported.

| SD | < ±2 % | ±2.44 % — target missed |

Two of five accuracy targets were missed. The coverage target — which the plan's own §7.4
calls the headline metric, "a wide honest interval beats a narrow lying one" — was met.

The temperature target was missed for a reason that is now understood and defensible: it is
genuine L1↔L0 model discrepancy, not error, evidenced by a matched-model control that
recovers `T_e` to 0.005 %. Removing it would mean tuning the analytic model to flatter one
configuration.

## 7. What to say, and what not to

1. Quote **4.31 % ± 2.44 % over 10 seeds at `cff2f94`, four channels, OES weighted
   `w = 1.2226e-3`**, with density −2.41 % and temperature +8.20 % next to it. Never the
   energy-flux number alone.
2. Coverage **8/10 at 90 %** is the headline metric, not the accuracy.
2a. Lead with the *mechanism*, not the number: fusing four diagnostics naively gives 25.6 %
   error at 0/10 coverage; an error-budget audit identifies one channel understating its
   uncertainty 28-138x; correcting for it gives 4.31 % at 8/10 **with all four channels
   retained**. That is a story about catching an inverse problem lying to itself, which is
   what the project is actually for.
3. The +8.2 % temperature bias is **measured model discrepancy**, evidenced by the L0→L0
   control at 0.005 %. Present it as a result, not an apology.
4. Never quote the 3-seed −0.04 % density figure. Never quote calibration-OFF rows.
5. State that LIF has no detector-noise model, that the probe comparison is against an
   *uncorrected* probe, and that everything is simulation-validated-against-simulation.
